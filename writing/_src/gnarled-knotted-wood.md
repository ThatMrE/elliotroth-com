---
no: 57
title: Gnarled Knotted Wood
date: 2015-11
shelf: poetry
book: allograft
---

Know where your hinges lie
said the carpenter to the apprentice
so you can tell what will make you bend

Working with my hands
I spent 6 days 17 hours and 43 minutes
making a frame of reference
And then on the seventh day
I burned it down

Jesus was a carpenter
Noah and Moses too
Abraham, Isaac, Jacob.
Most biblical men worked
with gnarled and knotted wood
burnt bushes and staves and stone
dead trees ringed with age
young saplings bent and buckled
to plowshares and pruning hooks

Impatience is a virtue and
you’re old enough to know when I stop counting seconds
it was the seventh seventh time you asked me
if I was alright
that I answered: “I feel uprooted”

In winter you can feel it the most
raised like flames against the sky
lashing out at heaven
limbs askew dancing in the dark
naked and bare

I have seen death at both ends
I have made coffee tables and bookshelves
from the rotted bark of almost embers
beating with hibernation

I have never built an ark

I’ve made myself deaf and blind
so that I can taste the truth as bitter as it is
that the faces of trees
turn inward in these cold months
and the hands of carpenters
become wood
