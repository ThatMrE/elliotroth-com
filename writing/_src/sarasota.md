---
no: 59
title: Sarasota
date: 2015-11
shelf: poetry
book: allograft
---

She said all old jazz musicians end up in Sarasota
before their notes fade in the air
because there’s something about that town
that pickles memories and floats them like fireflies
Waiting to be caught and set alight

In the steamy swamps south of New Orleans
you can hear the yell of a cornet call clear across the bayou
warning that the levee will break
to get back home before dark sinks you back under the mud
The sax screams back
calling down curses in
a hot red angry buzz
and if you listen close, the trombone frogs respond
hopping mad with heat
croaking about how they used to be princes
While the thrum of the bass soothes like dragonfly wings
flapping in time to the snap clap of the crickets
jumping to the snare drum beat
All these magic voodoo animals end up in Sarasota

the lazy smoke of Harlem
plays clarinet like a cigarette drag
syncopating city lights beneath
the neon-clad
it’s always twilight on the fire escapes
and the notes are always so blue
because it’s lonely in the city
Sarasota is calling to you

Chicago is a bragging city
With kick drum boom
and swaggering brass croon
like double-breasted suits
Double bass walks all over town
until it’s wood worn and tired
strings slapping against each other
like half-step siblings
the chromatic chrome street lamps
silhouetted like piano keys at midnight
taking the A-Train
to Sarasota

There’s only one street in the hot tin town
it’s one-way with a dead end
it twists and turns like a harmonica bend
and there’s a parade through every night
with Louie and Charlie and Duke and Ella
leading to the graveyard
It’s always a party there
Skeletons beat bones on
headstones like vibraphone keys
funeral jazz is slow and sweet
play til you’re on your knees
all old jazz musicians always end up
in Sarasota
