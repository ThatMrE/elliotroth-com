no: 37
title: Boxing Day
date: 2015-11
shelf: poetry
book: allograft
---

The days after Christmas are some of the saddest of the year.
The streets lined with discarded Christmas trees, relatives departed,
and you’re left with shredded wrapping paper and leftovers to eat.

It’s in those days that I think the entire world is holding its breath.
It’s as if the week between Christmas and New Year’s is forgotten time,
time on the brink of the precipice of a new age,
time lost and shoved under the rug because
the New Time will be so much better, so much cleaner, so much nicer.

I feel so bad for that forlorn felled forest lying on curbsides around the world.
I wish I could gather up the boughs of holly, the unkissed mistletoe, the poinsettias that were purchased from that marching band sale.
I wish I could gather them all up and have a trillion-watt generator and power all the tangled mess of wires and bulbs, all the spotlit nativity scenes.
I wish I could place them all so it seems like they’re having a party in the middle of this forest, that the
angels are doing the limbo with the three wise men.
Jacob and Mary have snuck off under the mistletoe.
Jesus is in the arms of an inflatable snowman.
Everyone has too much old eggnog and fruitcake.
Everyone wraps themselves up to pretend to be gifts to the world.

I want to make the world’s largest bonfire out of all the discarded Christmas trees.
It’d smell like a cleaning commercial.
We’d have people come for miles around to make s’mores.
It’d be so warm that we could stay outside all winter and not have to worry about shoveling snow.
We could dance around the fire in our new clothes and PJs and only our socks if we wanted to.

How come we always have to get socks as gifts?

The week spent in limbo, in celebration, in mourning for a spent Christmas ends with December.
You expect something different but nothing ever changes.
The Christmas trees are picked up by the garbage men and the lights are taken down.
The relatives pack up and friends go away.
It gets quiet again.
The trees grow.

We can only take so much celebration.
