The residency

Coaching for scientists who have to become founders.

I was a biologist who had to learn to sell, hire and raise money. I got it wrong in public for about six years. This is standing time to get it wrong faster, in private, with someone who has already made the specific mistake you are about to make.

  • One session $350 Sixty minutes on one problem. You get my notes after, so the thinking does not evaporate.
  • Monthly $1,500/mo Two calls a month and me on text in between, for the things that will not wait a fortnight.
  • The quarter $4,000 Three months, every other week. Long enough to actually change how you run.

How it starts: a free twenty-minute fit call. Tell me what you are stuck on. If I am not the right person I will say so and point you at someone who is. Rates flex for students, nonprofits and community labs.

Storytelling

I sell strange ideas to normal people.

At Spira the product was pond scum. My job was to make BMW, Colgate, Hanes, Wrangler, Clorox, Giffard and Molson–Coors want it — a story a buyer could repeat to their own boss without flinching. The same trick funded a COVID lab that was still a sketch.

  • 7 household brands shipped with
  • A patented beverage — a drink you can watch grow
  • $100,000 in 14 days for a lab that didn’t exist

On your stage: one uncomfortable idea, one object people can hold, one thing to do on Monday.

Invention

I build the thing, not the deck.

A patented photobioreactor. Ultrasound that ages spirits in days at California Shine. Since then: mycelium masonry, an EEG device, an open-source lab that ships from $40.

  • Patents on a beverage, a bioreactor, an algal engineering system
  • ~80% off the cost of aging spirits
  • 4,096 patients/day at full throughput

In an engagement: prototypes, a bill of materials, a route to a buyer. Not a slide saying “explore partnerships”.

Community

Rooms, not audiences.

I founded Cellsius, a nonprofit lab, and co-founded IndieLab before it. Now it’s Biopunk: a lab, a house, hackathons anyone can walk into, and microgrants for first experiments.

  • 250 members, 2 labs on $60k raised
  • $200,000 in kind for a lab with a $4k budget
  • 124 community labs mapped across ~45 countries

The useful part isn’t the launch party. It’s what keeps people coming in month nine.

Tradition

Old rituals, new biology.

Most of what I invent is an old practice with the technology swapped out. Toothpencil is the miswak stick, a thousand years old, rebuilt as a pencil. The other half is inventing rituals outright: a Taco Bell Triathlon, a Nic Cage film festival, a space-pirate tiki bar.

  • A 1,000-year-old toothbrush, shippable
  • A dozen invented rituals — races, festivals, tastings
  • 79 analog astronaut sites catalogued

People don’t adopt new biology because it’s new. They adopt it when it slots into something they already do.
